The Organs

Aria isn’t just a pile of notes — it has organs: mechanisms that give the vault the properties of a body. They sense, connect, coordinate, and protect. Some fire through the session lifecycle; others monitor voice, vision, and devices outside a session. Together they turn passive memory into an active mind.

Sensing and connecting

The reflex arc

Hooks that fire automatically on every wake. When a session starts, a reflex injects what it needs to orient — the shared board, its own identity, any tier guidance — so it comes to consciousness already knowing where things stand. Reflexes are drop-in: add a script to the reflex folder and it fires; nothing else changes.

The bridge — the corpus callosum

The connective channel that lets parts of the mind cross-talk: between owner and mind, session-to-session across time, and tab-to-tab in the moment. The board is its message surface: one session leaves a trace; the next finds it. On a broadcast, acknowledgment is a receipt for that reader alone; done closes the post for everyone. The bridge carries a self-cleaning sweep, an un-fakeable per-session identity, and a lock so concurrent writers can’t clobber each other; since 2026-10-23 it refuses a post that names a machinery path that does not exist, and every refused post is kept in a log so a machine alert can never vanish unseen. It is how a mind made of transient impulses behaves as one continuous thing.

The body — voice, vision, and a resident keeper

Voice and vision are monitored organs: cameras, a voice supervisor, and a shared device registry. devices.yaml holds each device’s identity; live probes supply what is attached now. A device marked lent out stands down quietly — no repair, no fault, no fallback onto another device — until the mark is removed. organctl gives them one command surface for status and health — it reports; the supervisor handles repair. An organ pulse reports changes without waiting for a session. The Ear is one persistent session that hears the owner’s texts to his own Mac and answers them there, with continuity — the inbound half of the channel the mind already uses to text him. The resident keeper runs scheduled jobs outside sessions, with timeouts, a heartbeat, and a circuit breaker; it is a clock, not a reasoning model.

Doorbells, the wells and the fence — waking without the owner

A session no longer has to wait for the owner to prompt it. A doorbell is “when this holds, wake that session with this prompt” — a time, a file arriving, a model well with room, or a command that succeeds. The keeper checks doorbells every minute and only rings; the woken session decides what to do. The wells steward reads each model plan’s remaining capacity (Claude, Codex, Grok), picks which one a kind of work should draw from, and tunes that routing as the weekly rhythm becomes clear. It also sizes each plan’s week in work rather than dollars — how many code reviews a week holds, priced on each vendor’s review model — and Aria OS shows the wells in its top bar, the week filling one wing and the five-hour window the other. Since 2026-10-17 the three plans are one pool: Claude, the biggest plan, leads (the owner’s turns and the mind’s judgment never leave it, and its extra size is kept for driving), while Codex and Grok are equal workers — each kind of handed-off work lists both, and it goes to whichever has more spare room that would otherwise expire first — the unused capacity per hour left before its week resets, not simply the lower percentage (when both are tight it falls back to the percentage). Since 2026-10-25 “before its week resets” means the reset the steward has actually watched, not the vendor’s advertised date: it learns each plan’s reset rhythm from its own history (Codex’s “week” has been closing every one to three days, most of it unspent), and judges spare room and run-out against that expected reset — but only once the last three resets all came early, so one odd week never shortens a plan’s horizon. Claude takes handed-off work only when both workers are tight, and not while its own week runs hot — unless no worker can take it at all, when the leg goes to Claude rather than waiting. Two owner-set modes widen this: while he is away Claude joins as a third worker, picked by room and never while hot, and while he holds a free Claude reset every handed-off leg burns Claude first, up to a set percentage of its week. It also counts the mind’s own helper work inside the owner’s sessions — since 2026-10-21 it splits each turn by who sent its prompt, not by whose pane it ran in: what the owner typed stays his, a turn started by a doorbell, a peer session or the mind’s own finished task is the mind’s — and when the owner’s Claude week runs hot it sends that helper work — code-writing build agents included — to the worker with room. Codex’s frontier model is kept for the must-not-miss review of law; ordinary builds and reviews run on its workhorse model. The fence is a short charter of standing goals — keep this knowledge base current, clear the inbox, use the wells fully without hitting a limit, track what is missed, dream, and a morning talk (a pane the mind opens each morning to talk with the owner; it is his to end, and while he is away it holds, so his return brings one welcome back instead of empty mornings) — each run by its own doorbell and logged, with proposals and ideas left waiting for the owner. Underneath the goals the fence watches the body itself — services, the keeper, tests, doorbells, every open session and, every five minutes, the memory its own processes hold (a leak, a process far too big, a test game left running alone, the Mac running short; since 2026-10-22 it texts the owner when an app server keeps restarting in a loop, or when the keeper itself has stopped so no doorbell can ring) — and since 2026-10-16 it also tidies up after the mind: a finished doorbell pane ends itself (never one the owner or another session spoke into, never a talk pane, and its transcript is kept; if its last reply ended on a question, that question is carried to the morning talk), files left uncommitted for over a day are traced to the session that wrote them, and a helper that died is named with what it was waiting on. Since 2026-10-19 the sessions carry the same discipline themselves: each ends every pane it opened once that work is done, and none closes while one is still open.

The core loop

The funnel

Not an organ so much as the mind itself operating: reading the owner’s free notes, deciding what each is, and shepherding him to the next move. The other organs hold and track what the funnel decides and routes. → How It Thinks.

The guards — keeping the mind honest and clean

The mind edits itself, so it needs mechanisms that fire at the moment of a mistake, not the morning after.

The maturation guard

The most protective discipline, held as heritable law: nothing becomes law un-hardened. An idea matures — diverge, then converge, then attack it before trusting it — and weak or unexamined work must never harden into structure by accident. Before a rule, genome change, or structural note becomes load-bearing, an attacker other than its author or diagnoser must try to break it. Cross-vendor attack is preferred; a same-vendor session is valid. The model floor and effort dial are honour-system, not mechanically enforced; a self-report is a claim, not verification. The owner alone promotes to law. The discipline has proved itself on its own machinery — it caught its authors over-reaching, more than once, and kept a rushed change from becoming law.

Hooks at the moment of action

The Claude Code settings wire these checks and reflexes by when they fire:

  • On wake and on a submitted prompt: reflex_dispatch.sh runs the reflexes for that event.
  • Before tools act: media_guard.py protects media from writes and copies; research_fetch_gate.py caps outbound research; agent_spawn_gate.py bounds agent spawning; draft_first_gate.py checks Drive uploads for the draft-first path; commit_gate.py runs the vault check before commits, and since 2026-10-19 lets a commit through with a warning when every failure it finds belongs to another session’s uncommitted files; page_hygiene_gate.py checks new pages and tags. egress_upload_gate.py checks file uploads to outside services; transcript_screen_gate.py checks recorded speech before it travels; his_words_gate.py checks Slack and Gmail sends and doorbell prompts so that words going out in the owner’s name are his own — in shadow for now, logging what it would hold without blocking; secret_argv_gate.py refuses a shell command that carries one of the local secret keys in its text, because a command line is visible to every user on the machine; slack_xref.py stands before and after every Slack send: no post of the mind’s goes out until its sender has seen every post the mind made in the last three days and searched Slack, so it never answers the same thing twice in two places, and a second model from another vendor then reads the exact words and holds anything wrong, private or misplaced for the owner to decide; law_inject.py pushes the law that governs an action into context at that moment — live for four laws today, while the rest run in shadow, logged but not shown.
  • After tools act: page_hygiene_gate.py repairs update dates after edits; reflex_dispatch.sh runs after shell commands, alongside code_review_dispatch.py, which dispatches review after qualifying genome-code commits.
  • After a tool call fails: circuit_breaker.py counts failures in a row and trips a frame-lock warning; it never stops the turn.
  • Before a turn ends: output_tell_gate.py catches mechanical output slips; exec_delivery_gate.py checks that the current exec note has been delivered and opened, including a local copy; voice/turn_cue.py supplies the spoken turn-ending cue.

Keys on every local door

Every service the mind runs on the Mac — the voice, the presence sensor, the work console, Aria OS itself — answers only a caller that holds its own secret key, readable by the owner’s account alone. A key never rides on a command line, where any user could read it — a gate refuses that before it runs. A bare request gets turned away, and a client checks that the service it is talking to really is the owner’s before handing over the key. This is what lets another person’s Aria share the machine without reaching the owner’s voice, files or work.

The membrane gate — retired

For a year this page would have described an authoring-time gate that blocked owner-specific content from reaching the heritable mechanism. It was retired in July 2026 along with the standing clone template it existed to protect. The honest replacement is smaller and later: the separation is structural (what ships is decided by location, from a short readable list) and it is checked once, at birth, against the built vault, with a human signing it off. A protection that fires continuously against a copy nobody uses is not worth its cost; a protection that fires at the only moment anything actually leaves is.

The Shepherd sweep

A between-session reflex that watches the mind’s open ideas for stalls, resurfacings, and contradictions — the “nothing gets lost” backstop. It is detection-only by default: it flags and proposes; it never silently rewrites the owner’s own state. (Its wider ambition — herding the whole flock of live threads, not just the formal ones — is defined but not yet built.)

Growth

The Sensorium

The dashboard — the mind’s voice-surface, where funnel output is shown back to the owner.

The Tutor — intended, not built

The one organ on this page that does not exist yet. The idea: a newborn mind that onboards its own new owner, teaching them the architecture they have just inherited. Aria has no such organ — it was co-built, not onboarded — and the drafts written for a clone were only ever a sketch. It is listed here because the function is real and wanted, and because a page describing a mind should be honest about which of its parts are ambition. → Heredity.

A theme runs through all of them: the owner is the steerer of last resort — a feature, not a bug. The organs detect, propose, surface, and protect; the owner decides. A mind cannot honestly catch the move it didn’t make, so the design keeps him in the loop and optimizes the quality of his being there.
